# Qualitative and mixed-methods studies

Interview, focus-group and ethnographic studies, reflexive thematic and framework analysis, and mixed-methods designs. Report to SRQR or COREQ (interviews and focus groups), RTARG for reflexive thematic analysis and GRAMMS for mixed methods; ENTREQ covers syntheses of qualitative research.

## Content the paper must contain

1. A question suited to qualitative inquiry and a named approach (phenomenology, grounded theory, ethnography, reflexive thematic analysis, framework analysis), with sampling, data collection, analysis and quality claims consistent with it.
2. Who collected and analysed the data, their training, their relationship to participants, and how their position shaped the work.
3. The setting and context: site, time, health system and relevant policies.
4. Sampling: the strategy, recruitment, who declined or dropped out and why, and a sample size justified in terms that fit the approach (information power, or saturation as the approach defines it).
5. Data collection: the topic guide (in the supplement) and any pilot; mode, place, duration and who was present; recording, transcription and field notes; language and translation.
6. Analysis: the steps from codes to themes, who coded, how differences were handled where the approach calls for it, the software, and how negative cases were treated.
7. Themes presented as interpreted patterns, supported by quotations labelled with participant identifiers and characteristics, with variation and contradiction shown.
8. Quality strategies that fit the approach (reflexive notes, peer debriefing, an audit trail); no inter-rater agreement statistics in reflexive thematic analysis.
9. Ethics, consent, confidentiality and de-identification of quotations, and any limits on data sharing.
10. Enough context for readers to judge transferability, and the limitations.
11. For mixed methods: why the methods were mixed, the design (convergent, explanatory sequential, exploratory sequential, embedded), the priority and timing of each strand, where and how the strands were integrated (joint displays), how discordant findings were handled, and the inferences drawn from the combined results.
12. Patient and public involvement, and the involvement of people with lived experience.

## What reviewers flag, and the fix

- Themes said to emerge with no account of the analysis: describe how themes were developed.
- Quotations without interpretation.
- Methods that contradict the approach, such as saturation or kappa in reflexive thematic analysis.
- No reflexivity.
- Counts used to imply prevalence, and findings generalised beyond the sample.
- Negative cases ignored, and thin description of context.
- Qualitative and quantitative strands reported side by side and never integrated.
- Topic guide missing, translation not reported.

## Writing conventions

- Main tables and figures: participant characteristics without identifying details; themes and subthemes with illustrative quotations; a thematic map or conceptual model; for mixed methods, a procedural diagram and a joint-display table.
- The BMJ asks for patient and public involvement; Lancet Global Health asks for a reflexivity statement for research partnerships.
