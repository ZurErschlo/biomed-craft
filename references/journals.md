# Journal styles

Flagship journals differ in format more than in substance. Follow the target journal's instructions for authors first; this file summarises what their published research articles do. Rules are set per journal, not per publisher: eClinicalMedicine and Lancet Regional Health, for example, do not follow The Lancet's number style.

If the user names no journal, keep the conventions the document already uses. For new text with no convention to follow, use Lancet style.

## Lancet family (The Lancet and its specialty journals)

- Title: `Topic: a [design]` with an article.
- Summary: Background, Methods, Findings, Interpretation, Funding. Background ends with `We aimed to…`; Findings open with dates and counts; Interpretation may recommend.
- Research in context panel with three parts (sections.md).
- British spelling (93% of Lancet text in the corpus).
- Verbs: `We did`, `was done`; `might` rather than `may`; `significant` used rarely.
- Numbers: mid-line decimal point (`0·72`); thin space in numbers of five or more digits (`10 000`); `to` in ranges that include a negative number; nested brackets inside parentheses: `3·81 per 100 person-years [95% CI 3·45–4·21]`.
- Intervals: `95% CI 1·20–3·40` with no comma after CI; label `95% CI` once, then bare intervals. Uncertainty intervals (UI) for model and GBD estimates, credible intervals (CrI) for Bayesian ones.
- P values: `p=0·03`, `p<0·0001`, lower case, no spaces.
- Proportions: `49 (2%) of 2609`.
- In-text pointers: `(figure 2)`, `(table 1)`, `(appendix p 5)`, `(appendix pp 14–17)`.
- Figures: `Figure 1:` plus a sentence-case noun phrase; legend below; abbreviations as `CI=confidence interval.`, alphabetical, repeated in every item. Panel letters bold upper case.
- Tables: `Table 1:` plus a noun phrase. In print the title sits below the table; in the submission file it sits above. First footnote declares the statistics (`Data are n (%) or median (IQR).`), then abbreviations, then marks in the order *, †, ‡, §, ¶, ||, **, ††.
- Discussion: one unheaded limitations block; final paragraph often prescriptive.

## NEJM

- Title: short noun phrase, no design label.
- Abstract: Background, Methods, Results, Conclusions (about 250 words), then funding and registration in parentheses. Conclusions restate the effect and do not recommend.
- American spelling. Few `we` (about 2·5 per 1000 words): the trial or the patients are the subject.
- Intervals: `(hazard ratio, 0.34; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.18 to 0.62)`, then `95% CI, 0.65 to 0.94`. P values: `P = 0.03`, `P<0.001`.
- Proportions: `482 of 2104 patients (22.9%)`.
- Pointers: `(Fig. 1)`, `(Table 2)`, `(Table S1 in the Supplementary Appendix)` at first mention, then `(Fig. S2)`.
- Figures: `Figure 1.` plus a Title Case title with a full stop; legend opens `Shown are…` or walks the panels (`Panel A shows…`). Abbreviations: `CI denotes confidence interval.` Panel letters bold upper case with Title Case panel titles; axis titles in Title Case.
- Tables: title at the top; first note (keyed by * on the title) gives general conventions (`Plus–minus values are means ±SD.`); units in row labels after an em dash (`Age — yr`); marks *, †, ‡, §, ¶, ‖.

## JAMA and the JAMA specialty journals

- Key Points (Question, Findings, Meaning) plus a structured abstract: Importance, Objective, Design, Setting, and Participants, Exposures or Interventions, Main Outcomes and Measures, Results, Conclusions and Relevance.
- American spelling.
- Intervals with a hyphen: `(HR, 0.49 [95% CI, 0.44-0.55])`. P values without a leading zero: `P = .009`, `P < .001`.
- Pointers: `(Figure 1)`, `(eTable 3 in Supplement 1)`.
- Figures: `Figure 1.` plus a Title Case noun phrase placed above the figure; a note below or beside it (`CI indicates confidence interval; HR, hazard ratio.`). Panel letters upper case in a box. Axis titles as `Quantity, unit`.
- Tables: Title Case title above; `No. (%)` spanners; row labels like `Age, median (IQR), y`; footnotes begin `Abbreviations: …`, then SI conversion factors, then superscript letters a, b, c.

## BMJ

- Title: `Topic: [design]` without an article.
- Abstract: Objective, Design, Setting, Participants, Main outcome measures, Results, Conclusions (reviews: Data sources, Eligibility criteria, Review methods).
- What is already known on this topic / What this study adds boxes. Patient and public involvement subsection in Methods. Discussion has subheadings.
- Spelling follows the authors. Intervals often spelled out: `adjusted odds ratio 7.12, 95% confidence interval 6.22 to 8.14`. P values `P<0.001`, `P=0.03`.
- Pointers: `(fig 1)`, `(table 2)`, `(supplementary table S1)`.
- Figures: `Fig 1 | Title.` with panels inline as `(A) …; (B) …`; abbreviations `CI=confidence interval;`. Tables: `Table 1 | Title.` often with the data format in the title line.

## Nature family (Nature, Nature Medicine, Nature Microbiology and the research journals)

- Abstract: one paragraph of 150–250 words: context, the gap (`However, … remains unclear`), `Here we show…`, two to four key numbers, a hedged closing sentence.
- Methods after the Discussion; Reporting summary; data and code availability statements.
- British spelling in Nature; American in Cell and Science.
- Numbers: comma thousands separators; `n = 12` with spaces; P values often in scientific notation (`P = 1.2 × 10⁻⁵`).
- Pointers: `(Fig. 1a)`, `(Extended Data Fig. 2a,b)`, `(Supplementary Table 3)`.
- Figures: `Fig. 1 | Bold title. Description.` Panel letters bold lower case (`a`, `b`) in the image and as run-in labels in the legend. Legends give n, the statistical test, and what points, bars and error bars show; 100–250 words.
- Tables: `Table 1 |` at the top; footnotes start with abbreviations, then letters.

## Cell and Science

- Cell: Highlights and an In brief summary; STAR Methods with a Key resources table. Legends have a finding-sentence title, one line per panel (`(A) …`), and a closing statistics block; `See also Figure S1` pointers.
- Science: `Fig. 1.` plus a title that is often a finding sentence; each panel description carries its n and test. Supplementary items: `fig. S1`, `table S1`.

## Annals of Internal Medicine

- `Figure 1.` plus a noun phrase; abbreviations written `CI = confidence interval.` with spaces. Supplementary tables cited as `Appendix Table 1`.

## Where the families agree

- No figure title inside the image; the title lives in the legend.
- Every abbreviation and every non-obvious graphical encoding is defined in each legend or table note.
- Present tense for what a figure shows, past tense for what was done.
- Group sizes in table headers; statistic formats declared once; no vertical rules in tables.
- Results open with numbers; Discussion opens with the principal finding.
