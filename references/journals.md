# Journal styles

Three layers apply to every manuscript. The general standard below covers wording and the conventions leading journals share. The journal layer adds the target journal's structure and format. The study-type layer adds the content and conventions of the design ([study-types.md](study-types.md)).

Journals differ mostly in structure and format; good wording is much the same in all of them. When the target journal is not settled, use the general standard alone. Follow the journal's instructions for authors first; this file summarises what its published research articles do. Rules are set per journal, not per publisher: eClinicalMedicine and Lancet Regional Health, for example, do not follow The Lancet's number style.

## General standard (no journal named)

- Structure: title, structured abstract (Background, Methods, Results, Conclusions), Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion. Results open with the flow of participants or the derivation of the sample, with numbers. The Discussion opens with the principal finding, then compares it with other evidence, gives strengths and limitations, and draws implications within the population and setting studied.
- Spelling: British or American, as the authors write, used consistently.
- Numbers: decimal point; comma thousands separators (`12,345`); `n of N (%)` for proportions (`482 of 2104 (22.9%)`); intervals with `to` (`95% CI 1.20 to 3.40`), which reads correctly with negative values; P values with a leading zero and two significant figures (`P=0.03`, `P<0.001`).
- Pointers: `(Figure 2)`, `(Table 1)`, `(Supplementary Table S1)`.
- Figures: `Figure 1.` and a sentence-case title; the title names what is plotted in clinical, epidemiological and modelling work, and states the finding in laboratory work. No title inside the image. The legend defines every abbreviation and encoding and gives n and the test.
- Tables: title above; the first footnote declares the statistics (`Data are n (%) or median (IQR).`), then abbreviations, then symbols. Group sizes in the headers; no vertical rules.
- Present tense for what a figure shows, past tense for what was done.
- Data availability and code availability statements, whatever the journal.

## Lancet family (The Lancet and its specialty journals)

- Title: `Topic: a [design]` with an article.
- Summary: Background, Methods, Findings, Interpretation, Funding. Background ends with `We aimed to…`; Findings open with dates and counts; Interpretation may recommend.
- Research in context panel with three parts: Evidence before this study (with the literature search: databases, dates, terms, language limits), Added value of this study, Implications of all the available evidence.
- British spelling. Verbs: `We did`, `was done`; `might` more often than `may`; `significant` used rarely.
- Numbers: mid-line decimal point (`0·72`); thin space in numbers of five or more digits (`10 000`); `to` in ranges that include a negative number; nested brackets inside parentheses: `3·81 per 100 person-years [95% CI 3·45–4·21]`.
- Intervals: `95% CI 1·20–3·40` with no comma after CI; label `95% CI` once, then bare intervals. Uncertainty intervals (UI) for model and burden estimates, credible intervals (CrI) for Bayesian ones.
- P values: `p=0·03`, `p<0·0001`, lower case, no spaces.
- Proportions: `49 (2%) of 2609`.
- Pointers: `(figure 2)`, `(table 1)`, `(appendix p 5)`, `(appendix pp 14–17)`.
- Figures: `Figure 1:` and a sentence-case noun phrase; legend below; abbreviations as `CI=confidence interval.`, alphabetical, repeated in every item. Panel letters bold upper case.
- Tables: `Table 1:` and a noun phrase. First footnote declares the statistics, then abbreviations, then marks in the order *, †, ‡, §, ¶, ||, **, ††.
- Discussion: no subheadings; one limitations block, often opening `Our study has several limitations.`; final paragraph may be prescriptive.

## NEJM

- Title: short noun phrase, no design label.
- Abstract: Background, Methods, Results, Conclusions (about 250–300 words), closing with funding and registration in parentheses: `(Funded by …; ClinicalTrials.gov number, NCT…)`. Conclusions restate the effect and do not recommend.
- Body: two to four unheaded introductory paragraphs, then Methods, Results and Discussion. Trials use Methods subheadings such as Trial Design and Oversight, Patients, Randomization, Procedures, End Points, Statistical Analysis. The Discussion has no subheadings and no Conclusions heading.
- Trial oversight paragraphs state that the authors vouch for the completeness and accuracy of the data and for fidelity to the protocol. The protocol and statistical analysis plan are published as a supplementary file.
- American spelling. Few `we`: the trial or the patients are the subject.
- Statistics: first use `95% confidence interval [CI], 0.65 to 0.94`, then `95% CI, 0.65 to 0.94`; `(hazard ratio, 0.83; 95% CI, 0.75 to 0.93)`; P values `P=0.03`, `P<0.001`. Comma thousands separators. `±` for means; interquartile range spelled out.
- Proportions: `482 of 2104 patients (22.9%)`.
- Pointers: `(Fig. 1)`, `(Table 2)`, `(Table S1 in the Supplementary Appendix, available with the full text of this article at NEJM.org)` at first mention, then `(Table S2)`.
- Figures: `Figure 1.` and a Title Case title ending in a full stop; legends often open `Shown are…` and define abbreviations as `CI denotes confidence interval.` Panel letters bold upper case.
- Tables: title at the top; notes keyed with *, †, ‡, §, ¶, ‖; recurring notes `Plus–minus values are means ±SD.` and `Percentages may not total 100 because of rounding.`; units in row labels after an em dash (`Age — yr`).

## JAMA and the JAMA specialty journals

- Key Points (Question, Findings, Meaning) and a structured abstract: Importance, Objective, Design, Setting, and Participants, Interventions or Exposures, Main Outcomes and Measures, Results, Conclusions and Relevance, and Trial Registration for trials. The design line opens `This [cohort study / economic evaluation / diagnostic study]…`.
- Body: Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, and a separate Conclusions section. The last subsection of the Discussion is Limitations. Methods state `This study followed the [CONSORT / STROBE / PRISMA / STARD / CHEERS] reporting guideline.`
- Results open with the sample and a demographic parenthetical: `(mean [SD] age, 56.9 [5.9] years; 1203 [52.1%] female)`.
- American spelling. Priority claims (`To our knowledge…`) are printed often.
- Statistics: `HR, 0.83 (95% CI, 0.75-0.93)` in running text and `(HR, 0.83; 95% CI, 0.75-0.93)` inside parentheses, with a hyphen in ranges and `to` when a bound is negative; P values without a leading zero and with spaces: `P = .009`, `P < .001`; `mean (SD)`, `median (IQR)`; thin-space thousands separators (`29 716`); `vs` without a full stop; the minus sign (−).
- Pointers: `(Figure 1)`, `(eTable 3 in Supplement 1)`. Supplement 1 is usually the trial protocol and Supplement 2 the statistical analysis plan.
- Figures: `Figure 1.` and a Title Case title with no full stop, placed above the figure; notes define terms as `CI indicates confidence interval; HR, hazard ratio.`
- Tables: Title Case title above; `No. (%)` spanners; row labels such as `Age, median (IQR), y`; notes open `Abbreviations: …`, then SI conversion factors, then superscript letters a, b, c.

## BMJ

- Title: `Topic: [design]` without an article.
- Abstract headings in sentence case: Objective, Design, Setting, Participants, Interventions, Main outcome measures, Results, Conclusions, and the registration. The Design field is a phrase (`Multicentre, double blind, randomised, placebo controlled trial.`). Systematic reviews use Data sources, Eligibility criteria for selecting studies, Data extraction and synthesis.
- What is already known on this topic and What this study adds boxes.
- Methods include a Patient and public involvement subsection. The Discussion has subheadings: Principal findings, Comparison with other studies, Strengths and limitations of this study, Policy implications, Conclusions.
- Spelling follows the authors (British is common). Hyphens are often dropped: `cost effectiveness`, `double blind`.
- Statistics: `hazard ratio 0.83 (95% confidence interval 0.75 to 0.93)`, later `(0.83, 0.75 to 0.93)`; `P=0.03`, `P<0.001`; space thousands separators; `versus` or `v`, never `vs`; standard deviation and interquartile range spelled out.
- Pointers: `(fig 1)`, `(table 2)`, `(supplementary table 2)`.
- Figures: `Fig 1` and a sentence-case caption; abbreviations as `CI=confidence interval`. Tables: `Table 1` and a caption that often states the format: `Values are number (percentage) unless stated otherwise.`

## Nature family (Nature, Nature Medicine and the Nature research journals)

- Abstract: one paragraph of 150–250 words: context, the gap, `Here we show…`, two to four key numbers, a hedged closing sentence.
- Methods after the Discussion; Reporting summary; Data availability and Code availability statements, with code deposited in a repository that issues a DOI.
- British spelling in Nature.
- Numbers: comma thousands separators; `n = 12` with spaces; P values often in scientific notation (`P = 1.2 × 10⁻⁵`).
- Pointers: `(Fig. 1a)`, `(Extended Data Fig. 2a,b)`, `(Supplementary Table 3)`.
- Figures: `Fig. 1 | Bold title. Description.` Panel letters bold lower case, in the image and as run-in labels in the legend. Legends give n, the statistical test, and what points, bars and error bars show; 100–250 words.
- Tables: `Table 1 |` at the top; footnotes start with abbreviations, then letters.

## Science

- Research Articles carry a structured summary written by the authors (INTRODUCTION, RATIONALE, RESULTS, CONCLUSION) as well as the abstract; the editor's summary and the one-line teaser are written by the journal. Reviews use BACKGROUND, ADVANCES, OUTLOOK.
- Reports often have no headings in the body. Research Articles open directly with the first results subheading and close with a Discussion. The full methods usually sit in the supplementary materials.
- Results subheadings are mostly topic phrases (`Characteristics of the new variant`), without a full stop; a finding sentence is kept for the main claim.
- Figures: `Fig. 1.` and a title, usually a noun phrase ending in a full stop; panels `(A)`, `(B)`; statistics stated in the panel text.
- Pointers in lower case: `fig. S1`, `table S1`, `movie S1`, `data file S1`.
- Data and materials availability sits with the acknowledgements: accession numbers, then materials, then code.

## Science Translational Medicine

- INTRODUCTION, RESULTS, DISCUSSION, MATERIALS AND METHODS. The first methods subsection is always Study design (objective, design, replicates and animals, randomisation and blinding, prespecified end points, rules for excluding outliers); the last is usually Statistical analysis.
- More passive voice than Science and almost no em dashes.
- Figures: `Fig. 1.` and a title ending in a full stop. Pointers `fig. S1` and `table S1`, sometimes capitalised.

## Cell

- Highlights: four or five lines, each a finding with its subject named, about 8–15 words, no full stop. In brief: about 30 words in the third person. A graphical abstract.
- Introduction, Results, Discussion, STAR Methods: Key resources table; Resource availability (Lead contact, Materials availability, Data and code availability); Experimental model and subject details; Method details; Quantification and statistical analysis.
- Results subheadings state findings more often than in other journals, especially in laboratory papers.
- Figures: `Figure 1` and a bold finding-style title with no full stop; one sentence per panel (`(A) …`); a closing statistics block (`n = 4 mice; ∗p < 0.05`); `See also Figure S1.` Supplementary legends end `, related to Figure 1`.
- Pointers: `Figure S1`, `Table S1`, `Data S1`, `Methods S1`, `Video S1`.
- Data and code availability as bullets: data with repository prefixes (`GEO: GSE…`), code with a DOI, and a closing line saying that any further information is available from the lead contact.

## Annals of Internal Medicine

- `Figure 1.` and a noun phrase; abbreviations written `CI = confidence interval.` with spaces. Supplementary tables cited as `Appendix Table 1`.
