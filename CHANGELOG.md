# Changelog

## 2.10.0

Released 2026-11-21.

- American spelling throughout the skill and in manuscripts (`-ize`, `-or`, `modeling`, `labeled`, `center`), unless the personal style file sets `Spelling: British` or `Spelling: Oxford`; journal editors apply their own house spelling, and `references/format.md` keeps one line on what each journal prints. New rule FMT-07 in `scan.py`, at review level, lists each spelling that departs from the target and leaves quotations, proper names and reference titles alone. `references/study-types/modelling.md` and `mendelian-randomisation.md` become `modeling.md` and `mendelian-randomization.md`, the design label `modelling` becomes `modeling`, and the `normalise_*` functions become `normalize_*`. `validate_package.py` fails on a British spelling outside code in the skill's own files.
- New script `check_references.py`: reads RIS, BibTeX or a list of DOIs and PMIDs, looks each entry up in Crossref and PubMed without sending an email address, and gives each one verdict: verified, fields differ (each one listed), ambiguous, not found, query failed or retracted (Crossref and PubMed both reported). With `--manuscript`, numbered citations and the reference list are checked both ways, in order of first citation and without gaps. When neither service answers, the lookups are reported as not done.
- New script `check_response.py`: new text quoted in a response letter appears sentence by sentence in the revised manuscript (Word or Markdown, tables included, tracked changes accepted), at the section, table or figure the letter names. Each sentence is found, differs (with the closest revised sentence) or missing; page and line numbers are reported as not checked.
- `check_numbers.py`: P values recomputed from t, z, χ² and F statistics and their degrees of freedom, within the rounding of the reported statistic; one-sided, corrected, exact and resampling tests and adjusted degrees of freedom become notes. A ratio interval that does not center on its estimate on the log scale is a note; uncertainty, credible, profile and bootstrap intervals are skipped. The GRIM test with `--grim-items K` when n × K is below 10 to the power of the decimals reported. PRISMA 2020 flow arithmetic for counts written as `label (n = N)`. Notes do not count under `--strict`.
- `check_preservation.py`: sentences that move from association to effect wording (`effect of`, `reduces`, `causes`, `prevents`) are listed, except in trials, and in meta-analyses of trials unless `--journal JAMA`; `predicts` counts outside prediction studies. For Word files, EndNote and Zotero citation fields are counted in both versions and the missing ones named, and tracked changes are checked by a round trip: rejecting them all gives the original, accepting them all gives the revision (`--accepted`), and other authors' changes stay.
- Code: new rule CODE-13 for comments and printed messages not in English, at must level; `Allow [CODE-13]` under `Document: code` in the personal style file lets in the researcher's own language, and names stay English. HYG-06 no longer covers comments. Figure and table code goes in its own numbered scripts after the analysis, reading saved output and never refitting, with the style and canvas width at the top of each; `style.R` is gone and CODE-06 flags shared style files (`references/analysis-code.md`, `references/project-folders.md`). The software version in the Methods is the one that produced the numbers, numbers in the text are read from output files, and a Python notebook lists and checks its packages in the first cell.
- `SKILL.md`: the description puts its main uses and trigger words first; the agent says how deeply each source was read (full text, abstract only, not obtained); every check is reported as passed, failed, not done or not applicable; before handling someone else's unpublished manuscript the agent asks whether the journal allows AI tools; the two new scripts are listed.
- Evidence (`references/evidence.md`): new sections on whether a source supports the sentence that cites it (the sentence split into its claims, six verdicts, a location for each supported claim), on checking references (title, first author, year and journal all match; a DOI that opens is not enough) and on retracted and corrected articles (each one removed, replaced or named as retracted, checked again before submission, with PubMed as the reference). Not searched, searched and not found, and not reported are kept apart. Disagreeing numbers get their likely causes listed before anything changes; published estimates are compared only when comparable; a new release of a source replaces the whole series; Methods and Results are checked both ways.
- Peer review (`references/review-response.md`): three jobs, each with its own section. Strictness follows the stage; several reviews of the researcher's own manuscript run separately; each concern is quoted and located after a search of the full text, supplement and protocol; a list of criticisms to leave out; checked concerns sorted into wording fixes, work for the researcher and concerns that do not hold. Reviewing for a journal needs the journal's permission for AI, stays local and keeps the comments to the editor apart. In replies, compound comments are split, each change is marked done, partly done or not done from the revised files, and up to seven key sentences are followed through each round.
- Plans and logs: checks before a plan is frozen (the estimand, intercurrent events kept apart from missing data, fixed decision rules, diagnostic tests that switch methods only by a written rule, multiplicity, one main analysis for each hypothesis, software defaults that change results); a trial's statistical analysis plan keeps its own version history (Gamble and colleagues 2017, items 4a–4c); a rerun that looks for the cause of a changed result changes one thing (`references/research-plan.md`, `references/execution-log.md`).
- Figures and tables (`references/figures-tables.md`): Nature's sizes (5–7 pt at final size, panel letters 8 pt bold); legends name no colors and keys explain each mark; contrast of 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for marks; chart types and axes (two y axes only for one quantity in two units, bars from zero, no pie or 3D charts); raw points when a group has fewer than about 10 observations; the canvas set to the journal's final width; long lists go to tables; one key order across panels; Chinese figures; alt text; spot checks against the plotted values; method details removed from a legend listed for the text.
- Sections and format: a list of the strongest reading and the likely over-reading of each main result before the Discussion; declarations provided, not applicable with a reason, or missing; AI use disclosed in the Methods or the acknowledgments; year ranges as the target journal writes them; citations and the reference list checked both ways; reference-manager fields and tracked changes kept intact; words counted by the journal's rules (`references/sections.md`, `references/format.md`). JAMA's rules on percentages, ratios, P values and causal language (`references/journals.md`).
- Study types: the design is judged from the Methods before a guideline is chosen. Trials: harms defined, collected and counted by participants with denominators (CONSORT Harms 2022); the effect size for the sample size from the minimal important difference, and simulated power checked under the null. Systematic reviews: pilot screening; two screeners, or a model as second screener checked by people; rules for automation and for recording model use; records awaiting assessment; records, reports and studies kept apart; the flow checked by `check_numbers.py`. Prediction: time-to-event models (censoring, competing risks, calibration by time point), fairness, external validation and open science. Modeling: calibration diagnostics (R-hat and effective sample size, BARG, boundary tests after Self and Liang, EPIFORGE, held-out validation, the types of validation). Economic: one price per draw, correlated parameters, transfers, reference cases, the mean as the main result, a net benefit table, `robust to`. Computational: differential-expression input, the enrichment background, GSEA ranking, gene-set versions. Diagnostic: agreement and measurement studies (GRRAS, Bland and Altman). Laboratory: significance stars. Mendelian randomization: colocalization, replication and the limits of causal wording.
- Workflow (`references/workflow.md`): ask before reading someone else's manuscript; recommend one option and offer two at most; independent plans from other models, sent only the named files; a section on handing work to another agent (which work, a model chosen by the cost of an error, three to five agents, the brief, a second checking pass where no other agent can run) and one on sending files to others. Documents: the length and audience of a talk asked first, result figures in slides kept as made, and a section on proofs. Project folders: handoff and archive READMEs, and the researcher's own folder names.
- `validate_package.py`: a reference file over 100 lines opens with a Contents line that names every section. `references/sources.md` lists the research skills borrowed from, the skill-authoring guides, and the new journal, reporting and methods sources.
- Fixes after an outside review of the new scripts and guidance, each reproduced first and covered by a test. `check_references.py` keeps the answers of the services and batches that did respond and never calls an entry verified when a lookup failed; it looks up in PubMed a DOI found by title, flags a PMID that PubMed does not know, reports a field that no record has, matches surnames by whole words and particles (`de la Cruz` matches `Cruz`; `Atlee` does not match `Lee`), keeps the brackets of older DOIs, reads Lancet half-year volumes and says when a manuscript has no reference list. `check_response.py` keeps comparison signs and powers of ten, does not count 0.8 as found in 0.82, drops citation superscripts and reads moved Word text only at its new place. `check_preservation.py` lists `associated with reduced` that becomes `reduced`, counts citation fields in footnotes and endnotes, and runs `--accepted` when the revision has no tracked changes. `check_numbers.py` rejects negative χ² and F statistics, reads P values such as `1e-5`, notes P values it cannot compute to full precision, finds a method named after the interval, treats a duplicate population as a reason for excluding a report, allows more studies than reports, and describes `--grim-items` as the number of items averaged into each score. `calibrate.py` drops control characters from DOIs read from PDFs. Guidance: censoring weights in external validation come from the evaluation data (prediction), boundary tests state their conditions (modeling), and figure lettering follows each Nature journal's own page.

- Figures (`references/figures-tables.md`, `SKILL.md`): a new part on marks, scales and reference lines. Reference lines are drawn only where they are defined and labeled with what they mark; one encoding for each variable and one meaning for each color; no overlapping translucent bands; compared panels share a visible scale; points for a few dozen observations; no series that only averages the others; one population and one set of denominators in a figure; the original authors' conclusions kept apart from the review's reassessment; sensitivity panels in the supplement; panel size by content; no gridlines along a categorical axis; a point lying on an axis checked against its source.
- Working through figures: a question from the researcher about an element is answered with a worked example from the data and then treated as a possible defect of the figure or legend, and the rendered figure is opened before a design is discussed.
- `references/workflow.md`: a new section on explaining a method to the researcher (what it is, how the project applies it, why it was chosen, a worked example; terms defined at first use; numbers from current outputs; a search before saying a step is standard, and a check that the Methods state it). Dropping records is the researcher's decision. After a change to a script the agent reruns it and everything built from it. When a method leaves the paper, its sheets, codebook rows, plan sections, scripts and supplementary items leave too.
- `SKILL.md`: before the closing lists, every request in the conversation and every step of the stated plan is checked, and a step left out goes under not done.
- Execution logs carry what the next full rerun must cover. Project folders gain a single `archive/` folder with dated subfolders in place of deleting unused files, and one-off scripts and reference material stay out of the data and analysis folders. A Python project may have its own style file (`references/execution-log.md`, `references/project-folders.md`, `references/analysis-code.md`).
- Prediction models (`references/study-types/prediction.md`): one saved split read by every script, language and outcome; nominal predictors as indicator variables selected as one variable, with one coding throughout; SHAP on the model chosen by cross-validation, on one stated scale; leave-one-site-out validation needs a site identifier, asked for at intake.
- Systematic reviews (`references/study-types/systematic-review.md`): the appraisal table keeps the tool's own answer codes and is checked against the characteristics table; statements about earlier reviews are checked against their full text; a result that first appears in the Discussion moves to the Results; the imprecision of prediction intervals from few studies is said once in the Methods; the extraction workbook holds only extracted values and their codebook.
- `references/sections.md`: an item is cited once in a passage, with the pointer last inside the parentheses. `references/format.md`: a section that holds only a placeholder is deleted, and page numbers are updated after a change of layout.

## 2.8.0

Released 2026-11-07.

- Proposals and redesigns: a proposed method, display item or structure names the comparable articles that use it, or says that none does. A new layout, grouping or set of categories for a figure, table or slide, and new sections, headings or template for a document, are shown as a rendered preview (two when the choice is one of taste) and applied once the researcher has chosen; defects that break a stated rule are still fixed without asking. Decisions in the log are read at the start of each session and applied to the work at hand, and a choice against the registration is reported as a departure from it (`SKILL.md`, `references/workflow.md`).
- Figures and tables (`references/figures-tables.md`): every panel, column and supplementary item earns its place, and leaving out the rest is proposed to the researcher; a figure too thin to stand alone becomes a table or a panel; axis titles name the exact quantity (denominator, period, absolute or relative change, scale); legends define every symbol, color and line style that no key explains and say which rows are shown and in what order; labels are parallel and keep one definition; a review uses one grouping scheme in every figure and table and shows no empty category unless the gap is the finding; keys, panel titles aligned at the top, tick labels, overlapping markers and insets; single-letter parameter symbols and defined line styles in model diagrams; PRISMA 2020 flow diagrams on the template that fits the review, without boxes that do not apply, with reasons for excluding reports ordered by frequency and no catch-all `other`; short column headers; reference curves computed from their definition; a style change applies to every figure; a figure uses one currency and price year.
- Systematic reviews (`references/study-types/systematic-review.md`): the reporting guideline follows the review's design; study features are coded into the classes of an established framework; a pooling rule is stated and applied to every group; the prediction interval is defined at first use as the range expected to contain the true effect in a new study; estimates for non-overlapping countries or groups in one study are pooled with a study-level random effect; a Bayesian random-effects model for two to four studies, with its prior justified and varied; appraisal checklists for economic evaluations and cost-of-illness studies; pooled costs and burden follow comparable reviews on grading; a method that was not used is mentioned only where a reporting item asks for it or as a change from the protocol.
- Cost-of-illness and economic burden studies have their own section in `references/study-types/economic.md`: no reporting guideline is agreed, so the items of the consensus appraisal checklist are covered; health burden first; the total cost with its components; welfare losses beside the costs and never added to them, since willingness to pay is assumed to include lost earnings and out-of-pocket costs; one currency in each figure; international dollars as `I$` or `Int$`, one form throughout.
- Modeling (`references/study-types/modeling.md`): each scenario or combination is justified; a cost-effectiveness plane keeps dominated strategies; a calibration residual is never plotted as a cause, and its size is reported in the supplement, or in the main text when it is large.
- Documents (`references/documents.md`): protocols and consent forms stay on the sponsor's or committee's template; slide titles state the message; new sections on talk scripts and on documents in Chinese.
- Word and online documents are edited where they live and directly, with tracked changes when the researcher asks or the journal wants a marked copy, under the author name the researcher gives; co-authors' parts follow the rules for shared documents; the saved file keeps the original's fonts, bold, headings and background colors outside the edits. The style file may hold the researcher's own name for this, and no other people's names (`references/personal-style.md`).
- Supplementary methods extend the main Methods without restating them (`references/sections.md`). The `SKILL.md` description adds figures, tables, slides, 改图, 改表 and 写讲稿, so that agents call the skill for figure and slide work.
- `references/sources.md` lists the methods guidance behind the new review and costing rules: the Cochrane Handbook chapter on meta-analysis, Bender and colleagues (2018) and Röver and colleagues (2021) on very few studies, `bayesmeta`, the JBI method for reviews of economic evaluations, the cost-of-illness appraisal checklist, Turner and colleagues (2019) and Robinson and colleagues (2019).

## 2.3.0

Released 2026-10-27.

- Study-type detection is right for about seven in eight hand-labeled articles from leading journals, up from four in five. ## 2.0.0

- Principles and confirmation points for working with the researcher, from the research question to the reply to reviewers (`references/workflow.md`), and the rule that every task ends with what was done, not done, done wrong and still to decide.

- Three layers: a general standard, the journal's structure and format, and the study type. `scan.py --type` sets or guesses the study type; rule levels and reference groups follow it.
- Research plans, execution logs, analysis code, comments and project folders (`--doc plan`, `--doc log`, `--doc code`), and evidence, data and consistency (`references/evidence.md`).
- New checks: `check_numbers.py` (percentages, intervals, abstract against text) and `check_fulltext.py` (retrieved PDFs against the reference list).
- A larger calibration corpus: open-access publisher versions from Europe PMC (NEJM, JAMA journals, BMJ, Science, Sci Transl Med, Cell, Lancet journals, Nature Medicine) read as JATS XML, with study designs labeled and per-design groups; rules corrected for the false positives the new articles showed.
- Leading journals instead of flagship journals throughout; no article counts in the skill text; peer review and replies to reviewers (`references/review-response.md`).
