# Randomised trials

Parallel, cluster, crossover, factorial, non-inferiority, adaptive and platform trials. Report to CONSORT 2025 and its design extensions (cluster, non-inferiority and equivalence, crossover, stepped-wedge, pilot, adaptive, CONSORT-AI, CONSORT-C for participants up to 19 years); plan with SPIRIT 2025 and a statistical analysis plan. Reviewers judge bias with RoB 2.

## Content the paper must contain

1. The design, the framework (superiority, non-inferiority or equivalence) and the allocation ratio. A non-inferiority margin is justified on clinical and statistical grounds.
2. Each outcome defined by its measurement, analysis metric, method of aggregation and time point, matching the registry and protocol.
3. The effect estimated: the analysis population for each analysis and, when the trial used the framework, the estimand (population, treatments, endpoint, summary measure, and the strategy for intercurrent events such as rescue treatment or death).
4. The sample-size calculation with every assumption and its source, and the interim-analysis and stopping rules.
5. Sequence generation (with any restriction or stratification), allocation concealment, who enrolled and assigned participants, and who was masked and how.
6. Missing data by arm, the assumption behind the main handling (for example missing at random with multiple imputation), and a sensitivity analysis under a different assumption. Last observation carried forward is not used.
7. For every outcome: the number analysed, the result in each arm, and the effect with its 95% CI; for binary outcomes, the risk difference as well as the relative effect.
8. How harms were defined and collected (systematically or spontaneously), and participants with events by arm, serious events and withdrawals due to harms included.
9. Each intervention described well enough to replicate (TIDieR), and what was delivered: fidelity, adherence, dose received and concomitant care, by arm.
10. Prespecified analyses kept apart from post hoc ones; subgroup effects as interaction tests; multiplicity control stated.
11. Recruitment and follow-up dates, and why the trial ended or stopped early.
12. The open-science items: registry, number and registration date (before the first participant); where the protocol and statistical analysis plan can be read; the data-sharing plan; funding, the funder's role and conflicts of interest; patient and public involvement, or a statement that there was none.
13. An interpretation that weighs benefit against harm, and limitations that cover bias, imprecision, generalisability and multiplicity.

Changes made after the trial started go where CONSORT 2025 asks for them: in the checklist, or a table in the supplement comparing registered, protocol and reported outcomes. The Methods describe the trial as it was run, and a statement that the primary outcome or eligibility changed stays in.

## What reviewers flag, and the fix

- Spin after a null primary result: lead with the primary result and its CI, and label everything else exploratory; no `trend towards`.
- Non-significance read as no difference: read the CI against the minimal important difference; claim equivalence only within an equivalence or non-inferiority framework.
- Over-read subgroups: few, prespecified subgroups with a hypothesised direction, interaction tests, and a credibility assessment (ICEMAN).
- P values for baseline differences: remove them and comment on clinically important imbalances.
- Per-protocol or modified intention-to-treat as the main analysis without justification: make the intention-to-treat analysis primary; in non-inferiority trials show both.
- A composite outcome driven by its softest component: report every component.
- Early stopping for benefit: give the stopping rule and say that the effect is likely overestimated.
- Results generalised beyond a narrow population: report the screening funnel and how representative the participants are.

## Journal requirements beyond CONSORT

- Lancet: protocol and statistical analysis plan at first submission; a Randomisation and masking section; the primary outcome in the Summary as a between-group difference with its CI; numbers at risk under Kaplan–Meier curves; Research in context; Role of the funding source.
- NEJM: original and final protocol and analysis plan; P values only for prespecified analyses with multiplicity control, otherwise estimates with intervals; a supplementary table on the representativeness of participants; the statement that the authors vouch for the data and for fidelity to the protocol.
- JAMA: Key Points; protocol and analysis plan as Supplement 1 and 2; counts of participants approached, eligible, randomised and completing in the abstract; who determined race and ethnicity and how; no P values in baseline tables.
- BMJ: patient and public involvement, plans to share results with participants, the guarantor's transparency declaration, data sharing.

## Writing conventions

- Results open with flow and dates: `Between March 4, 2021, and June 30, 2023, 1412 patients were screened and 806 were randomly assigned (403 to each group; figure 1).`
- The Discussion opens with the main result, the design and the population: `In this trial of adults with …, [treatment] reduced [outcome] by 4·2 percentage points (95% CI 1·1–7·3) compared with placebo.`
- Main figures and tables: the CONSORT flow diagram by arm; Table 1 by arm without P values; the outcome table with absolute and relative effects; Kaplan–Meier or cumulative-incidence curves with numbers at risk; a forest plot of prespecified subgroups with interaction P values; the harms table.
