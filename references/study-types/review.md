# Narrative and clinical reviews

Narrative, clinical and state-of-the-art reviews, Seminars, and reviews in laboratory journals. No consensus reporting guideline exists; SANRA is the usual appraisal scale, RAMESES covers realist reviews, and a review that uses systematic methods reports to PRISMA or PRISMA-ScR ([systematic-review.md](systematic-review.md)).

## Content the paper must contain

1. The question, scope and intended readers, and why the review is needed now (new trials, a changed guideline).
2. The search: databases, terms, the date of the last search, language limits, and how evidence was selected and weighted.
3. Claims resting on the strongest evidence (systematic reviews, trials, guidelines), with primary sources cited and the strength of evidence labelled.
4. Numbers: absolute rates, effects with CIs, sensitivity and specificity for tests.
5. Conflicting and negative evidence, and the open controversies.
6. Synthesis by question or theme, never study by study.
7. A search updated close to submission.
8. Established evidence kept apart from mechanism and from expert opinion.
9. Practical recommendations in proportion to the evidence, consistent with guidelines or with the difference explained.
10. The review's own limitations: selection that was not systematic, and the authors' perspective.
11. Conflicts of interest. The Lancet does not publish reviews by authors with relevant company ties in the previous three years.

## What reviewers flag, and the fix

- Selective citation: describe the search and include the contrary evidence.
- Outdated evidence: update the search.
- Clinical claims resting on other reviews or on animal and mechanistic data: cite the clinical evidence, or label the claim as mechanistic.
- No appraisal of study quality.
- Relative effects only: add absolute effects.
- Recommendations beyond the evidence or out of step with guidelines.
- A textbook restatement with no new synthesis.

## Journal formats

- Lancet Seminars and Reviews: an unstructured summary of about 150 words, and a Search strategy and selection criteria panel.
- NEJM Review Articles: no abstract; three or four unheaded opening paragraphs; topical headings; a closing Summary or Conclusions. Clinical Practice articles open with a case vignette and a Key Clinical Points box, then The Clinical Problem, Strategies and Evidence, Areas of Uncertainty, Guidelines, Conclusions and Recommendations.
- JAMA Narrative Reviews: Importance, Observations, Conclusions and Relevance in the abstract; a Methods section naming the databases, the search dates and how papers were selected, with the search terms and numbers in the text or the supplement; a Discussion and Observations section organised by topic (pathophysiology, clinical presentation, diagnosis, treatment, prognosis); limitations numbered First, Second, Third; a closing Conclusions section.
- BMJ State of the Art Reviews: an unstructured abstract, then Introduction, Sources and selection criteria, epidemiology and topical sections, Emerging treatments, Guidelines, Conclusions; boxes for a glossary, questions for future research and how patients were involved.
- Science reviews carry BACKGROUND, ADVANCES and OUTLOOK in their summary. Cell reviews keep an Introduction and then sections named after mechanisms, closing with Concluding remarks or Outlook. Nature Reviews add Key points.

## Writing conventions

- Reviews are built by argument, not by IMRaD: each section answers one question, and the last looks forward.
- Reviews use `we` rarely and carry more hedges and contrasts than research articles; contrasts still need both sides stated with evidence.
- `In this Review` may open the scope statement, and a one-sentence roadmap may close the introduction.
- Words such as `intriguing`, `myriad` and `intricate` (LEX-11), and the stock phrases of LEX-02, are flagged for judgement in reviews rather than as must-fix.
- Main figures and tables: evidence-summary tables (study, design, n, population, result), a diagnostic or treatment algorithm, a mechanism schematic, a table of recommendations and open questions.
